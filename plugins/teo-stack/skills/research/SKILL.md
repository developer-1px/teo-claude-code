# Research

Research exists to be used. Owner's rule (2026-11-16): «리서치는 반드시 써먹어야
하기 때문에 리서치를 하는 거야.» Every section must serve the action the
requester will take next.

## 1. Start from why, then pick the items

- Quote the requester's words. Infer why they want this research: which decision
  or next action it feeds and what is blocking it now. Find this in the brief,
  the requesting session, or the issue before searching; mark what stays unknown
  instead of inventing stakes.
- Choose the research items from that intent. Keep an item only if its answer
  could change what the requester does. State the chosen items and why at the top.

## 2. Core: as-is → to-be, and why the to-be

For each relevant competitor or reference product:

- **as-is → to-be**: what the part looked or worked like before and after, with
  version or date.
- **why they chose the to-be**: the problem they named and the trade-off they
  accepted. Prefer their own explanation (redesign posts, changelogs, release
  notes, talks, public issue or PR discussions). If the reason is inferred, say
  so once for that claim.
- Skip products that did not change the part in question, unless their stability
  is itself the finding.

Then synthesize the **evolutionary convergence direction**: which independently
evolved products became more alike, under which selection pressures (the shared
"why"), and how stable it is: stable (standards, guidance, and adoption agree),
emerging (independent experiments move together), contested (credible products
choose differently under different constraints), or absent. One vendor, or
several articles repeating it, is not convergence. Include counter-signals.

## 3. Insight → action items

From the reasons and the direction, state what it means for us and propose
concrete action items: what to change, where, the first step, and how we will
know it worked. Each action cites the change that motivates it. Order by value
to the requester's intent. Actions are proposals; research alone does not
authorize code changes, issues, or external actions.

## 4. Images and diagrams throughout

- Every as-is → to-be case carries a picture next to its paragraph: before/after
  screenshots (official images or captured screens), or a diagram of the change
  when no image exists.
- The convergence direction gets a diagram: a timeline or map of who moved where.
- Each picture has a one-line caption: what to look at, source, date. Attach the
  image files with the result; do not collect them in an appendix.

## Evidence

Prefer primary sources and read the ones behind central claims. Cite next to the
claim with a short paraphrase. Include dates when they affect the reading.
State conclusions plainly; do not prefix paragraphs with fact/inference labels.
Put unresolved facts where they matter, briefly. Stop when the action items have
adequate support; do not keep searching to manufacture consensus.

## Output order

1. 왜 이 리서치를 하나 — requester's words, inferred intent, chosen items
2. 결론 — convergence direction and the actions it implies, in a few lines
3. 사례 — as-is → to-be · why, one section per product, each with a picture
4. 수렴 방향 — diagram, selection pressures, stability, counter-signals
5. 액션 아이템 — table: action · motivating change · first step · check
6. 출처
